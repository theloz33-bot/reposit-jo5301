01 — The skill

One page, four rules, one skeleton

The attached references decide the format; the format decides which fields exist. Getting this wrong is the single most common reason a video prompt comes back flat — not the adjectives.

1.1

Three laws that outrank every template

01

One role per reference image

A reference carries one job: this face, this outfit, this place. Stack a full-body sheet into a face slot and the model averages the two, which is how a character ends up wearing someone else's jaw. Face, outfit and location get their own image and their own sentence in the prompt.

02

One camera move, one action per shot

Two moves in one sentence is read as two shots. The model will split it, and the split lands mid-sentence with the background repainted. If a cut is needed, cut it in the edit, where it is free.

03

The prompt is a plan; the timeline is the truth

A timecode inside a shot is a wish. What the model honours is the end state of each beat: where the hand is, what the face is doing, which way the body has turned. Write the end state, then trim to the beat in the editor and let the picture land on it.

1.3

The six fields, in this order

Ref2VA · field names, spelling and the blank line between fields stay unchanged

FieldWhat goes in it

retention_analysisWhat stays and what changes, per shot. fully_preserved / partially_preserved.

1.4

Speaking: the tag, the language, the budget

TAG

(S1), (S2) — never renumbered

A character who speaks or sings gets a speaker ID. It is assigned once and kept across every part of the song, so part four does not introduce a new voice for the same person.

LINE

<d>[Korean] line</d>

Every sung or spoken line sits inside <d> with a language tag and nothing else: no stage directions, no translation, no mouth notes.

MOUTH

State the closed mouth

A mouth that should be shut is stated shut, with a time: before 2.30 s her mouth is closed. Left to chance, the model starts singing at frame one.

DIALOGUE LINE — THE SHAPE
(S1) sings: <d>[Korean] <THE LINE, AND ONLY THE LINE></d>. Before 2.30 s her mouth is closed.
5–8 s · one shot120–250 words
10–15 s · with cuts250–500 words
Length targets are a budget, not a minimum. A prompt inside a prompt — a Russian doll — counts against the same budget and is read in order, so the nested block has to earn its words.
1.5

The negative defense: three layers, in this order

why "no X" alone fails

A negative line steers a sample; it does not remove the affordance. A model that renders text well will invent text, and a signboard is rendered because the scene asked for one — so subtract by design first, and by denial last.
L1

Design the place

Choose a location where the unwanted thing has no reason to exist. A riverbank at pre-dawn has no advertising. Distant buildings are kept as haze silhouettes on purpose, so there is no readable facade for the model to letter.

L2

Describe the surface positively

Every surface gets a positive description — plain, unmarked, matte, unlabelled — and the absence rides on the object itself: a plain white rectangular kite with one solid circle near its top edge, no letters or symbols. Geometry in words first, then one short absence.

L3

Four short negatives, at the very end

Text · logos and signage · subtitles and watermark · duplicated faces. Four items is a boundary; a longer list is read as a mood and starts costing you the rest of the prompt.

One named exception beats a blanket denial. Only <NAME 1> may wear glasses; no other character wears glasses at any point works where "no glasses" invites four background characters in spectacles.
1.6

Anatomy of a shot prompt — eight beats

BeatWhat it holds
SubjectAge, wardrobe, one distinguishing detail. The same words in every shot.
ActionA change of state, not an adjective. "Looks down, exhales, lifts her eyes" renders as performance.
PlaceTwo or three concrete details. Wet asphalt, a steam vent, hanging cables.
CameraMove, height, lens. One move per shot.
LightingName the source, not the mood: a lamp on the left, a cool window behind.
GradeTwo colours and a contrast note.
FormatAspect ratio and where the subject sits in frame.
NegativesFour short items, last line, always the same four.
02 — Camera

Thirteen moves, three axes

Do not stack tags at the end of a sentence. Write the move as the action of the sentence, then let one tag confirm it. Amplitude and speed are worth writing only when they are not the default.

MoveWhat it doesWhen to use it
Push InThe camera body travels forward; parallax included.The moment a character decides.
Pull OutThe camera body travels back; the world arrives.The last line of a section.
Zoom In / OutFocal length changes; the frame flattens.Almost never — it reads as a photo.
Pan Left / RightThe lens turns from a fixed spot.Revealing what is beside the subject.
Truck Left / RightThe camera slides sideways, parallel to the subject.Walking beside someone.
Tilt Up / DownThe lens turns up or down from a fixed spot.Following a kite, a tower, a fall.
Pedestal Up / DownThe whole camera rises or drops.Revealing a crowd from a face.
Arc ShotA curve around the subject.Two people circling each other.
Tracking ShotFollows a moving subject.Running, driving, falling.
Static ShotNothing moves but the subject.One held face, one verse.
Shake Slightly / StronglyHandheld instability.Impact, panic, a live crowd.
POVThe subject's own eyeline.A phone screen, a doorway, a fall.
Roll CW / CCWThe frame rotates on the lens axis.A dream, a loss of control.
2.1

Amplitude and speed

two modifiers, not two moods

with small amplitude

The frame reframes a little.

with large amplitude

The frame is rebuilt.

at slow speed

One move takes most of the clip.

at fast speed

The move lands in the first second.

2.2

Tag-to-sentence mapping

the move is the verb; the tag is the confirmation

EXAMPLE 01
The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed toward the folded letter in her hands. [Push in]
EXAMPLE 02
The camera pans right with large amplitude at fast speed, revealing the open doorway. [Pan right]
EXAMPLE 03
The camera holds a static shot as the runner exits the frame. [Static shot]
Two moves in one sentence is read as two shots, and the split lands mid-sentence with the background repainted. If the cut is wanted, cut it in the edit.
2.3

The shot spec, one line

move, height and lens in a single clause is a complete camera instruction

TEMPLATE
<SUBJECT: age, hair, wardrobe, one detail> <ACTION: verb, verb, verb — a change of state>. The camera <move> with <small|large> amplitude at <slow|fast> speed toward <target> [<Move>]. Lighting: <source> as key, <source> as rim, no fill. Grade: <two colours>, <contrast note>. <Aspect ratio>, subject on <placement>. No text, no logos, no watermark, no duplicated faces.
FILLED
A woman in her late twenties, black bob, mustard cardigan, stands at a doorway and looks back over her shoulder. The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed toward her face [Push in]. Lighting: doorway light as key, cool window as rim, no fill. Grade: amber and teal, controlled contrast. 16:9, subject on the right third. No text, no logos, no watermark, no duplicated faces.
03 — Templates

Ten templates that survived the render

Each one is a workflow, not a paragraph: a still to generate, a sentence to paste, or a line to keep unchanged across every cut. Replace what is inside angle brackets; leave the rest alone.

T01

The Identity Lock

IMAGE · 16:9 · ONE SENTENCE, PASTED INTO EVERY PROMPT
CHARACTER SHEET PROMPT
Character reference sheet, photorealistic, soft natural light, plain light-grey studio background. Three views side by side of the same person: front portrait, full-body front, full-body three-quarter. <NAME>, a Korean woman in her late twenties with a shoulder-length straight black bob and side-swept bangs, wearing an oversized mustard-yellow knit cardigan over a plain white T-shirt, light-wash straight jeans and plain white canvas sneakers, no jewelry. Calm, gentle expression, mouth closed. Plain unmarked clothing, no text, no logos, no labels anywhere.
THE LOCK STRING — PASTE VERBATIM, EVERY CUT
<NAME>, a Korean woman in her late twenties with a shoulder-length straight black bob and side-swept bangs, wearing an oversized mustard-yellow knit cardigan over a plain white T-shirt, light-wash straight jeans and plain white canvas sneakers, no jewelry

Why it worksNineteen cuts, and the face held, because the description is one sentence with nothing optional in it: age, hair, the exact garment colours, and one absence (no jewelry). The sheet gives the model a face; the lock string stops it drifting. Write the lock once, paste it without editing — and when two cuts disagree, rebuild the key still, because editing the prompt does not fix a changed face.

T02

The Prop Lock

IMAGE + VIDEO · ANY OBJECT THAT REPEATS
HERO PROP
a plain white rectangular Korean shield kite with a round hole in its center and a single solid indigo circle painted near its top edge, no letters or symbols, trailing a long thin white string to a simple wooden kite reel
CROWD VERSION
plain kites in soft solid colors (white, sky blue, pale yellow, coral), no letters, logos or patterns

Why it worksA prop that appears more than twice is a character, and it drifts faster than a face does. Two things make this lock work: geometry described in words (rectangular, centre hole, one circle, near the top edge) and an explicit absence (no letters, no symbols). Copy the same two lines into the key still and the video prompt; a kite that gains a logo between cuts is the same failure as a face that changes.

T03

The Location Plate

IMAGE · 16:9 · NO PEOPLE, ONE PLATE PER TIME OF DAY
BACKGROUND PLATE
Photorealistic cinematic wide shot, 16:9, background plate. A wide grassy riverbank park on the Han River at pre-dawn, soft blue-grey light with a faint peach glow on the horizon, low mist over calm river water, a long steel arch bridge silhouetted in the far distance, distant apartment towers softened into haze, empty lawn. No people, no silhouettes, no hands. Cinematic 35mm film look, fine grain, no text, no signage, no logos, no billboards, no readable letters anywhere.

Why it worksOne plate per location and time of day, generated empty, then reused under every key still in that section. It fixes horizon height, weather and colour, so five separately generated cuts feel like one afternoon. The last sentence is the important part: distant buildings are haze silhouettes on purpose, because a model that renders text well will happily invent a signboard on a readable facade.

T04

The Reference Map

IMAGE · THREE REFERENCES · FACE, OUTFIT, PLACE
KEY STILL PROMPT
Cinematic film still, 16:9, in the exact visual style of the approved concept art. REFERENCE MAP: @Image 1 supplies <NAME>'s face only. @Image 2 supplies the outfit only. @Image 3 supplies the location only. Never show a reference sheet, collage, or studio backdrop. <NAME>, Identity lock: <PASTE THE IDENTITY LOCK STRING>. She stands in the doorway with one hand on the frame, looking back over her shoulder. Location: <PASTE THE LOCATION PLATE DESCRIPTION>. The location from @Image 3 must fill the frame behind her; no white, blank, or empty background. Composition anchors: subject on the right third, doorway light falling across her face, sky visible in the upper left. Single frame, no split panels, no collage. Avoid: text, letters, numbers, signage, logos, subtitles, watermark, distorted faces, missing background, plain white or solid-color void, extra people, costume mismatch.

Why it worksThree references, three jobs, stated one by one. Without that map the model treats every image as a style reference and averages them, and you get a character in a collage. The two sentences doing the heavy lifting are "supplies the face only" and "must fill the frame behind her" — one stops the face sheet leaking into the wardrobe, the other stops the studio backdrop following the character into the scene.

T05

The 10-Second Cut

VIDEO · I2VA · ONE SHOT · 120–250 WORDS
VIDEO PROMPT
Start from @Image1 as the exact first frame: at 0.00 seconds of the target video, @Image1 is fully referenced as [Shot 1], preserving its composition, lighting, character and wardrobe exactly.

integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic, a medium shot exactly as in @Image1. <LOCK STRING> stands at frame left holding <PROP LOCK>. In the opening seconds (about 0-5 s) <first change of state>. Midway (about 5-10 s) <second change of state>. By the end (about 10-15 s) <where the hand is, where the eyes are, what the mouth is doing>. The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed [Push in]. Lighting stays constant.

overall_soundscape: <wind, footsteps, cloth, breath>. No voices.

non_diegetic_music: N/A

Constraints: one continuous shot with no cuts or transitions. No one speaks or sings; lips remain closed. No text, letters, numbers, logos, signage, subtitles or watermark anywhere in the frame. Face, hair, hands and clothing stay stable and identical to @Image1.

Why it worksThe alignment sentence is not decoration: it tells the model that frame zero is a fact, not a mood, and it is the difference between a clip that starts on your still and one that starts near it. Three beats, each ending in a physical state, and no timestamp inside the shot — a timecode here is a wish, an end state is an instruction.

T06

The Lip-Sync Cut

VIDEO · Ref2VA · 30 S · SIX STILLS + THE AUDIO
VIDEO PROMPT
REFERENCE MAP: @Image 1-6 = key stills C01 to C06, in order. @Image 7-9 = face locks for the three singers. @Audio 1 = the vocal track for this part only (song 0.000-29.950 s).

integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] 0.00-5.62 s: <action>. The camera cranes down with large amplitude at slow speed [Crane down]. Lighting stays constant, warm low sun. The older woman with a low, warm, slightly husky voice (S1) sings: <d>[Korean] <LYRIC LINE>.</d> Before 2.30 s her mouth is closed. [Shot 2] 5.62-11.24 s: <action, one change of state>. <S2 line appears here.>

overall_soundscape: <foley only - cloth, a gate hinge, rubber gloves>. No other voices, no extra lyrics.

non_diegetic_music: Provided by @Audio 1. Do not invent a different song.

Constraints: one continuous shot per section, no invented cuts. Synchronise every sung line to @Audio 1. Generate the complete 30.00-second clip; do not end early. Settle and hold a clean seam frame for the next part; no sudden close-up finish.

Why it worksSeven of these, thirty seconds each, cut together into a three-minute video. Two lines make the seams invisible: the explicit "holds a clean seam frame" at the end, and "Provided by @Audio 1, do not invent a different song" — without the second, the model writes its own music under your vocal. Speaker IDs never change between parts, and a mouth that should be shut is stated shut, not left to chance.

T07

Six Shots in 15 Seconds

VIDEO · Ref2VA · 6 SECTIONS · 2.5 S CADENCE
6-SECTION PROMPT
subject_definitions: <Subject 1> is the young woman in <Picture 1>, in her twenties, black hair tied back, grey cropped hoodie, black leggings, black gloves. <Subject 2> is the man in <Picture 2>, black tee, grey shorts, shin guards.

summary: The target video is a fast-cut 15-second live-action kickboxing sparring sequence in six quick shots, escalating from a cautious standoff to a spinning back kick, with every strike blocked or dodged and no injuries.

retention_analysis: <Subject 1> (appears in [Shot 1]-[Shot 6]): fully_preserved - face, hair, hoodie, leggings, gloves; partially_preserved - the tote bag is set down in [Shot 1] and not carried after. <Subject 2>: fully_preserved - face, tee, shorts, guards.

detailed_description: Live-action, cinematic, natural daylight in an empty plaza. [Shot 1] A wide shot between rows of folding chairs; she sets her tote bag on a chair and steps forward. [Shot 2] At 00:02.500, the shot cuts to a side-on medium shot at chest height; she throws a low kick into a jab-cross-hook and he blocks with both forearms. The camera shakes slightly with each impact. [Shot 3] At 00:05.000, a low-angle shot; his high roundhouse passes over her back as she ducks. [Shot 4] At 00:07.500, the camera arcs around them with large amplitude at fast speed as her spinning back kick drives him back. [Shot 5] At 00:10.000, a medium-wide shot; he catches her jump kick and both reset. [Shot 6] At 00:12.500, a static close shot; her high kick stops a hand's width from his face, then they touch fists. The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed.

overall_soundscape: Footfalls on stone, gloves thudding, sharp breaths, one shouted count. No music.

non_diegetic_music: N/A

Why it worksFight footage is where a 15-second clip usually turns to mush, because six ideas are asked for at once. The retention block is the fix: naming what must survive and what is allowed to change gives the model a reason to keep the two faces. And the safety rule in the summary ("every strike blocked or dodged, no injuries") is not squeamishness — it stops the model inventing a contact frame that reads as a glitch.

T08

The 30-Second Spot

PLANNING + IMAGE · ONE PRODUCT BEAT · NO LOGO
PLANNING PROMPT
Give me three 30-second commercial directions for <PRODUCT>, each with a hook in the first two seconds, a single product beat between 8 and 12 seconds, a turn at 18-22 seconds, and a final frame that holds for 0.6 seconds. For each direction: the world in one line, the protagonist, the arc, the one shot that sells it, and the risk of it failing.
CUT PROMPT — ONE PER CUT
REFERENCE MAP: @Image 1 supplies <NAME>'s face only. @Image 2 supplies the outfit only. @Image 3 supplies the location only. Never show a reference sheet, collage, or studio backdrop. <NAME>, Identity lock: <LOCK STRING>. <ACTION>. The brand colour appears only as light: <e.g. lime-green glow bleeding across wet chrome>, no logo, no lettering, no packaging. Composition anchors: <subject placement, negative space>. Avoid: text, letters, numbers, signage, logos, subtitles, watermark, extra people, costume mismatch.

Why it worksThree directions before one frame is generated, because a commercial fails at the idea, not at the render. The product appears as light, never as a logo: a brand colour on wet chrome reads as a product shot without giving the model a sentence to render as lettering. Hold the last frame for 0.6 seconds and the brand colour is the last thing the viewer sees, which is the whole job.

T09

The Continuity Line

VIDEO · ANY COSTUME OR PROP THAT CHANGES MID-FILM
STATE LINE — KEY STILL
Costume state: no apron and no gloves yet; sleeves rolled to the elbow.
STATE LINE — VIDEO
Costume state: no apron in Shot 2, apron tied from Shot 4, red rubber gloves from Shot 6, sleeves pushed up from Shot 8 and still pushed up in the final shot. Only <NAME 1> may wear glasses; no other character wears glasses at any point.

Why it worksA static description cannot hold a costume that is supposed to change. State the progression with cut numbers attached, and state it in the video prompt as well as the still, or the apron appears in shot two and the gloves vanish by the chorus. The glasses line is the same idea in reverse: one exception, named, with everyone else denied — otherwise four background characters invent spectacles.

T10

The Motif Escalation

PLANNING · A COUNT THAT GROWS WITH THE LYRIC
PLANNING PROMPT
The chorus repeats a number: one, then two, then ten, then a hundred, then a thousand. Build the shot list so the picture counts with it, and so the count is set by the key still rather than by the video prompt. For each cut give: the count, which characters are in frame, how much of the frame is sky, the one change of state, and the camera move. Never let a cut contain more copies of the motif than its count, and never add a copy in the video prompt.
PER-CUT LINE
Count in frame: 10 kites, all plain, all in the upper third of the frame. No additional kites may appear during the shot.

Why it worksNineteen cuts, one motif multiplying from one kite to a thousand, and it held because the count lives in the key still and the video is forbidden from adding to it. Choose a motif with no face and a simple background — a kite against sky is stable at a thousand; a thousand faces are not. The count is also the edit: each number in the lyric is a cut point you can plan before you render anything.

04 — Library

Short patterns, ready to paste

One shot each, with the camera, the light and the aspect already decided. Draft at low resolution, keep the take that keeps the motion, then upscale that take only — re-rendering at high resolution with an edited prompt is how a face changes between shots.

L01 · Tracking · 6 s · 16:9

Rain Street Walk-Through

PROMPT
A lone figure in a black translucent raincoat walks toward camera down a narrow neon-lit alley at night, heavy rain, wet asphalt reflecting pink and cyan signage, steam rising from a vent. Camera: slow backward tracking shot at eye level, 35mm lens, slight handheld float. Lighting: motivated by red neon on the right and cyan signage overhead, rim light on the coat, no fill on the face. Color: teal and magenta, deep blacks, fine grain. No text, no logos, no lens flare, no camera shake beyond a gentle float.

Ask for a backlit subject on purpose. When the face stays in shadow, you can cut the same character into five different shots and nobody notices the drift.

L02 · Drone · 8 s · 16:9

Aerial Tower Push-In

PROMPT
Aerial night shot of a dense megacity with a single broadcast tower in the center, thousands of lit windows, traffic light trails on an elevated highway. Camera: smooth drone push-in toward the tower, starting wide at 1km and ending medium, constant speed, no roll. Lighting: city glow from below, dark blue sky, tower beacon blinking red. Color: cold blue shadows, amber street lights, high contrast. Volumetric haze over the skyline. No text, no watermarks, no warping buildings.

For a title card, render this once and freeze the last frame. A held aerial frame with type over it looks intentional; a moving one fights the text.

L03 · Portrait · 8 s · 16:9

The Held Close-Up

PROMPT
Close-up of a woman in her late twenties, dark hair tied back, no makeup, wearing a grey knit sweater, sitting at a kitchen table at night. She looks down, exhales, then lifts her eyes to the lens without smiling. Camera: locked-off close-up, 85mm, very shallow depth of field, no movement. Lighting: single warm practical lamp from the left, cool window light from behind, soft shadow on the right cheek. Natural skin texture, visible pores, no beauty retouching. No text.

Write the emotional beat as an action sequence — looks down, exhales, lifts her eyes. Prompts that describe a change of state produce performance; adjectives produce a pose.

L04 · Follow · 6 s · 16:9

Walking Away, Handheld

PROMPT
A man in a dark coat walks away from camera along an empty riverside path at dusk, hands in pockets, shoulders slightly hunched. Camera: handheld follow from behind at shoulder height, 40mm, gentle sway, keeping him in the lower third of the frame. Lighting: flat blue dusk, distant city lights beginning to come on, no direct sun. Color: desaturated blue-grey, soft contrast, light grain. No text, no other people, no cut.

Keep the subject in the lower third and you get a free title area. Shoot the same walk at dusk and at night and you have a verse and a chorus.

L05 · Two-shot · 7 s · 16:9

Two People, One Frame

PROMPT
Two people sit across from each other at a small table in a dim apartment, a single lamp between them, neither speaking. One reaches for the other's hand and stops halfway. Camera: slow lateral dolly right, 35mm, both faces in frame, equal weight. Lighting: lamp as the only key, faces lit from below-center, deep shadow behind. Color: amber and deep brown, warm but heavy. No text, no extra people, no smiling.

The unresolved gesture — reaching, then stopping — is the most reliable way to get an emotional frame out of a model. Do not ask for a hug; ask for almost one.

L06 · One take · 8 s · 16:9

Spotlight Solo, No Cuts

PROMPT
A single performer stands in a tight spotlight on a dark stage, eyes closed, singing, one hand on the mic stand. Camera: slow 180-degree orbit at waist height, 40mm, constant speed, performer centered. Lighting: one hard top spotlight, everything else black, thin rim from the back. Color: warm white key against pure black, high contrast, no color grade. No text, no audience, no strobe.

An orbit is the most reliable expensive-looking move: one subject, one axis, constant speed. Ask for it at waist height, where faces distort least.

L07 · Vertical hook · 4 s · 9:16

Face Fill, First 0.5 s

PROMPT
Vertical close-up of a person's face filling the frame, eyes already on the lens, a small movement of the head as if they just turned toward camera. Slight handheld motion. Lighting: soft window light from the left, dark background. They raise one eyebrow. Camera: handheld, 24mm equivalent, subject's eyes at the upper third. Color: natural, warm skin, no filter. Frame is 9:16, subject centered, safe from UI overlays. No text, no zoom.

Vertical hooks have no establishing shot. Start on the face, mid-expression, and the scroll stops for the same reason a conversation does.

L08 · Transition · 4 s · 9:16

Before / After Whip

PROMPT
Vertical shot of a person standing in a plain room, then a fast whip pan right and the same person now in full stage costume in a different location, same pose. Camera: one continuous whip pan, 26mm, motion blur at the pivot, both halves of the shot vertical 9:16. Lighting: identical soft front light on both sides of the whip so the cut hides inside the blur. Color: neutral. No text, no dissolve.

The trick is matched framing, not a clever effect. Put the camera at the same height and distance for both halves and the whip does the rest.

L09 · Vertical hook · 3 s · 9:16

Object Drop, Beat Sync

PROMPT
Vertical shot of a headphone falling in slow motion onto a dark surface, catching a single rim of light, dust particles in the air, then landing exactly as the frame cuts. Camera: locked-off low angle, 50mm, shallow focus. Lighting: one hard side rim on the falling object, black background, dust visible in the beam. Color: black, chrome, one warm highlight. Frame is 9:16. No text, no hands, no splash.

Cut on the landing frame and the drop becomes the kick drum. This is the single most reliable three-second hook in short-form audio.
